Chapter I. Project Background

Executive Summary

         In recent years, the Philippines has faced challenges in public project implementation, especially in areas
such as flood control infrastructure and confidential fund allocation. According to the Commission on Audit (COA
annual reports, 2023), billions in public funds remain unaccounted for or underutilized due to limited
transparency. These problems have made people lose trust in the government, slowed down progress, and caused
delays in important public projects.

         In a 2023 audit report, COA also mentioned the Office of the Vice President for spending over 375
million in confidential funds, raising concerns about accountability and proper fund usage (Inquirer, 2024).
Meanwhile, flood control projects have drawn criticism from civil society groups and religious leaders, who called
for greater transparency and shame-free governance practices (ABS-CBN News, 2025).

         To address these gaps, Project Track PH was created as a centralized digital platform for monitoring
government implementation activities. It provides real-time updates, milestone tracking, and automated
reporting to improve transparency, accountability, and responsiveness. The system helps government teams
coordinate better and also allows the public to see progress, report delays, and take part in monitoring.

         Project Track PH also aligns with the Philippine Development Plan 2023­2028, which states that the
government aims to strengthen digital transformation and citizen participation in governance (NEDA, 2023). Its
modular design allows it to scale across various services, including flood control monitoring, infrastructure
rollout, and social service delivery.

         Ultimately, Project Track PH is more than just a system; it's a governance tool. It empowers both
government agencies and citizens to ensure public funds are used properly and that promised projects are
completed as planned.

Mission

         The Mission of Track PH: Government Project Implementation and Monitoring is to authorize a unified
projects registry and a database that will provide a digital platform that allows real-time monitoring and
evaluation of government project implementation, promote accountability, enhance transparency, and provide
resources to ensure that every peso works for our Filipino citizens.

                                                                                                                                                   4
Vision

         The Project TrackPH Vision is to make project monitoring in the Philippines more transparent, organized,
and easy to track through a digital platform. It aims to improve project management that supervises and builds
public trust by making information clear and reliable to everyone.

1.1 Introduction

         Government project transparency in the Philippines continues to face serious challenges. Investigations
into flood control programs and infrastructure spending have revealed corruption, inflated budgets, and poor
documentation (MSN News, 2025). In 2025, congressional reports showed that unprogrammed appropriations
ballooned from 158.7 billion to 531.7 billion before being partially vetoed, raising concerns about
accountability and fund misuse (Philippine Daily Inquirer, 2025). These issues have not only delayed public
services but also discouraged foreign investment, with U.S. stakeholders citing corruption as a major barrier to
economic engagement (Romualdez, 2025).

         To respond to these problems, government agencies and lawmakers have started pushing for digital
reforms. The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) launched the Integrity Chain platform to track
infrastructure spending using blockchain technology (Zaldarriaga, 2025). Senate Bill 1330 also proposes a
blockchain-based national budget system that records disbursements and procurement activities in real time
(Bacelonia, W.,Philippine News Agency, 2025). These efforts show that there is a growing demand for systems
that offer clear, tamper-proof records and public access to project data.

         As part of this progress, our group developed the Milestone and Timeline Monitoring microservice under
Project Track PH. This microservice allows agencies to define project milestones, set deadlines, and monitor
progress through automated alerts and visual dashboards. It also generates timestamped reports that can be
used for internal audits or shared with oversight bodies and the public.

         This microservice can improve project coordination, reduce delays, and strengthen public trust. It
supports real-time tracking, milestone verification, and proactive reporting, making it easier for agencies to stay
on schedule and for citizens to hold them accountable. By designing the timeline to show real progress, the
system helps ensure that government projects are not only planned but completed with integrity.

1.2 Project Purpose & Description

1.2.1 General Objective

         The general objective of the Milestone and Timeline Monitoring microservice is to develop a digital
solution that enables government agencies to track project milestones, monitor implementation timelines, and
generate progress reports in real time. It aims to improve transparency, coordination, and accountability in public
project execution by providing a standardized and accessible monitoring framework. The system is designed not
only for internal use but also for public visibility, allowing citizens to see actual project progress and hold agencies
accountable.

1.2.2 Specific Objectives

    1. To establish a standardized framework for tracking project milestones and completion timelines across
         different agencies and sectors.

    2. To provide real-time visibility of project progress for stakeholders, including implementing teams,
         oversight bodies, and the public.

    3. To identify and flag delays, risks, and bottlenecks early, allowing timely intervention and corrective action.
    4. To enhance accountability and transparency in government project implementation through timestamped

         records and automated reporting.
    5. To support evidence-based decision-making by generating data-driven monitoring reports that reflect

         actual project status and performance.

1.3 Project Scope and Delimitation

         The Milestone and Timeline Monitoring microservice was created to help improve how government
projects are tracked in the Philippines. Many public projects face delays, missed deadlines, and poor coordination.
This system was designed to solve those problems by giving agencies and citizens a clear way to monitor project
progress and timelines.

         The microservice focuses on tracking milestones and deadlines for public sector projects. It provides
real-time updates, sends automatic alerts when there are delays or changes, and generates reports in a standard
format. These reports help government teams, oversight bodies, and the public understand how projects are
moving. Citizens can also access project updates through a public interface, which helps promote transparency
and public involvement.

                                                                                                                                                   6
         This microservice does not check budgets, spending, or financial records; that scope belongs to another
microservice. It also cannot enforce rules or give penalties. Its role is to monitor and report, not to take legal
action. Lastly, the system only covers government-led projects. Private sector projects are not included, so the
focus stays on public programs and services.

1.4 Significance of the Project

Government Agencies: This microservice provides a reliable tool for monitoring project implementation across
various government departments. By offering real-time tracking and standardized reporting, it helps agencies
improve coordination, reduce delays, and ensure that milestones are completed on time. It also supports
transparency by making project data visible to oversight bodies and the public, helping agencies meet
accountability standards.
Citizens and Communities: The system allows ordinary citizens to view project progress through a public
interface. This promotes transparency and encourages public participation in monitoring government initiatives.
When people can see updates and report delays, it builds trust and helps ensure that public funds are used
properly. Communities benefit from faster, more organized project delivery.
Policy Makers and Oversight Bodies: The data collected by the microservice can support evidence-based
decision-making. Lawmakers and auditors can use the reports to identify bottlenecks, assess agency
performance, and recommend improvements. This helps strengthen governance and ensures that policies are
based on actual implementation data.
Future Developers and Researchers: This project serves as a foundation for future systems that aim to improve
government transparency and digital monitoring. Developers can build on its modular design to expand features,
while researchers can use its data to study project efficiency, public engagement, and digital governance models.

Chapter II. Review of Related Literature

2.1 Local Literature

The Importance for Transparency and Accountability in Philippine Public Spending

         Literature consistently highlights that the push for digital project monitoring in the Philippines is driven by
persistent issues of corruption and low public trust (DBM, 2016). The Philippine Development Plan 2023­2028
emphasizes that good governance, transparency, and accountability are crucial enablers for economic
transformation (Philippine Development Plan, 2023).

         The Open Budget Survey (OBS) and related fiscal transparency assessments have historically pointed out
gaps in government reporting, such as a lack of detailed comparisons between actual spending and
appropriations, and information not being presented in a way that citizens can easily access and understand
(DBM, 2016). This lack of access to underlying data hinders public accountability and the capacity for citizen
participation in monitoring and evaluation (M&E) activities (Open Government Partnership, n.d.-a). Therefore, a
core function of modern M&E systems is to disclose project performance to the public to promote transparency
and allow citizens to provide feedback (Open Government Partnership, n.d.-b).

Digital Initiatives for Project Monitoring and Evaluation

         The Philippine government has actively pursued e-Government and digital transformation strategies to
enhance public administration (Department of Budget and Management, n.d.; Treceñe, 2021). The Digital
Information for Monitoring and Evaluation (DIME) Project is a significant example of a national initiative
leveraging modern technologies like satellites, drones, and geotagging to monitor large-scale government
projects (Open Government Partnership, n.d.-a). The DIME project is unique in its goal to simultaneously monitor
financial metrics and provide actual project images and status to enhance transparency (Open Government
Partnership, n.d.-a).

         However, a key challenge identified in the literature is the fragmentation of M&E systems, as different
government agencies often create their own stand-alone systems, making it difficult for citizens to track various
projects across different applications (Open Government Partnership, n.d.-a). This fragmentation limits the
impact of accountability efforts and reduces citizen participation (Open Government Partnership, n.d.-a).

                                                                                                                                                   8
Leveraging Real-Time Technology for Infrastructure Verification

         Specific academic research has demonstrated the efficacy of real-time technologies in improving project
oversight. A study focusing on the Philippine Rural Development Project (PRDP) found that the use of geotagging,
a form of location-specific data collection, provided real-time data that significantly improved remote oversight
and accountability for infrastructure delivery (Magwili et al., 2025). Key findings from this research confirmed
that geotagged photos effectively validate physical progress, reducing the need for frequent, resource-intensive
site visits and enabling faster decision-making (Magwili et al., 2025).

         Furthermore, the conceptual application of Blockchain technology has been explored for government
processes in the Philippines to enhance transparency and verifiability (Manzano & Tiongson, 2023). Blockchain's
defining properties of immutability and tamper-evident record-keeping are seen as mechanisms to secure
government data, strengthen institutional accountability, and enhance the credibility of transactions (Ocampo,
2024; World Economic Forum, 2020). However, some academic analysis suggests that the implementation of
high-cost technologies like blockchain may not fully address the root institutional and political causes of
corruption (Koghut, 2025).

Participatory Monitoring of Infrastructure Projects in the Philippines

         Project DIME (Digital Imaging for Monitoring and Evaluation) is a government initiative
developed by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM) that integrates satellite imaging,
drone technology, and geotagging into a public transparency platform intended for real-time monitoring
of government infrastructure projects (Open Government Partnership, 2019). This system empowers
citizen participation by allowing the public to validate project milestones, provide feedback, and
contribute to solving issues such as procurement delays and contractor performance problems. By
facilitating transparency and accountability through technology-enabled, participatory monitoring,
Project DIME enhances timely problem detection and improves overall project management quality.
Recent upgrades incorporate mobile platforms and data analytics to further enhance accessibility and
data use efficiency, reflecting continuous efforts to refine project oversight processes (DBM, 2025).

         This study is directly relevant to Project Track PH's goals, outlining a proven model that leverages
digital tools and citizen engagement to strengthen milestone and timeline monitoring. It supports
Project Track PH's approach to promote transparency, collaboration, and technology utilization in
government project implementation monitoring.

                                                                                                                                                   9
Integrated Monitoring and Evaluation Systems in Philippine Local Governance

            Recent studies emphasize the evolution and challenges of monitoring and evaluation (M&E)
systems within Philippine local government units (LGUs). Lalaguna (2025) highlights the necessity of
needs assessment in e-governance, stressing that effective project management requires aligning digital
solutions with institutional demands and citizen needs to promote transparency and efficiency. This is
demonstrated in the Philippine Government Electronic Procurement System (PhilGEPS), which
integrates stakeholder engagement, data-driven planning, resource allocation, and performance
monitoring. Nonetheless, challenges around system integration, training, and data security persist,
indicating areas for improvement.

        Similarly, Diokno-Sicat (2020) explores the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS), which
gathers data at the barangay level to track development indicators related to poverty and welfare.
CBMS empowers local planners and officials to perform evidence-based development planning, yet it
faces operational challenges such as capacity limitations and sustainability concerns within LGUs. The
synergy between these systems underscores the importance of combining data systems with
participatory governance to strengthen public sector monitoring and evaluation.

         Both Lalaguna (2025) and Diokno-Sicat (2020) examine frameworks integral to Philippine
government monitoring practices, reinforcing the need for multi-layered, participatory, and data-centric
approaches. These frameworks support Project Track PH by illustrating effective models of structured
monitoring and governance while highlighting technological and institutional challenges that must be
addressed for improved government project implementation.

Digital Innovations and Capacity Building in Government Project Monitoring

       The integration of digital technology in government project monitoring has become a key strategy
in enhancing transparency, data accuracy, and accountability. Reyes and Santos (2023) evaluated the
adoption of digital dashboards and real-time monitoring technologies in Southeast Asian utilities,
illustrating significant improvements in operational efficiency and stakeholder engagement. Meanwhile,
the Open Government Partnership (2024) detailed the Philippines' DIME portal as a transformational
tool allowing public access to project status, budget details, and interactive feedback mechanisms. The
portal's success depends heavily on active civil society participation and proper implementation by
government agencies to translate data disclosure into meaningful accountability. These studies

governance to be impactful.

           The continuous evolution and promotion of platforms like Project DIME resonate with Project
Track PH's mission to institutionalize digital monitoring mechanisms, empowering both public officials
and citizens to collaborate in tracking government project milestones and timelines effectively.

2.2 Foreign Literature

Optimizing Project Governance Through Stakeholder-Centric Milestone Scheduling

         The study by Vyneesa Writer (2025) focuses on how stakeholder engagement in milestone
scheduling can improve project governance and reduce implementation delays. It highlights that
traditional project tracking systems often ignore stakeholder participation, which leads to poor
communication and misaligned goals. By aligning project milestones with stakeholder feedback and
decision checkpoints, the study emphasizes that collaboration and transparency are essential for timely
and effective project delivery.

         This research relates to Project Track PH: Milestone and Timeline Monitoring as both aim to enhance
project visibility and accountability through structured milestone tracking. Like the stakeholder-centric
framework, Project Track PH encourages shared responsibility among government agencies and
citizens, ensuring that project progress is transparent, data-driven, and participatory.

Controlling a Multibillion Project Portfolio ­ Milestones as Key Performance Indicator
for Project Portfolio Management

         The study by Eik-Andresen et al. (2016) explores how milestone tracking can be used as a key
performance indicator (KPI) in managing large-scale project portfolios. The research analyzed data from
more than 2,000 milestones across a seven-year period in a multibillion-dollar portfolio of public
projects. Findings revealed that even when individual projects experience delays, consistent milestone
monitoring allows portfolio managers to anticipate setbacks, adjust budgets, and maintain overall
financial stability. The study emphasizes that success in project portfolio management does not always
depend on individual project completion but on the ability to manage collective milestone performance
efficiently.

                                                                                                                                                 11
The research further identifies recurring patterns of delay that managers can use to predict
performance trends and adjust their strategies accordingly. Through transparent reporting systems,
milestone data serves as an early warning mechanism for potential risks and deviations, supporting
proactive decision-making and long-term project sustainability. The authors also highlight that
transparency, real-time data interpretation, and continuous governance play crucial roles in ensuring
accountability within complex portfolios.

Stakeholder-Driven Milestone Modeling (SDMM): A Framework for Reflecting
Approval Cycles, Oversight Protocols, and Decision Checkpoints in Project
Management

         The study by Pratik Bhikhubhai Panchal (2020) presents the Stakeholder-Driven Milestone
Modeling (SDMM) framework, which integrates stakeholder participation into milestone planning to
improve project governance. Unlike traditional milestone systems that only track technical progress,
SDMM incorporates approval cycles, oversight protocols, and decision checkpoints to ensure that all
stakeholder expectations are considered throughout the project. This method enhances transparency,
accountability, and coordination among project teams, reducing risks and delays caused by
miscommunication or unaligned goals.

         This framework directly relates to Project Track PH: Milestone and Timeline Monitoring because
both emphasize collaboration, transparency, and real-time visibility in project implementation. By
promoting stakeholder engagement and structured milestone tracking, SDMM supports the same
principles of participatory and accountable governance that Project Track PH seeks to achieve in
monitoring public projects.

Data-Driven Project Monitoring: Leveraging Dashboards and KPIs to Track
Performance in Technology Implementation Projects

         The study by Andikan Udofot Umana et al. (2022) explores how data-driven project monitoring
using dashboards and key performance indicators (KPIs) enhances transparency, accountability, and
decision-making in complex technology implementation projects. By integrating analytics platforms
with project management tools, the researchers developed a real-time monitoring framework that
enables organizations to visualize progress, detect risks, and measure performance accuracy across
various project phases. The study found that such dashboards and KPIs improve issue detection by up to

                                                                                                                                                 12
50% and overall efficiency by 35%, making them vital tools for modern project governance and
stakeholder coordination.

This research supports the goals of Project Track PH: Milestone and Timeline Monitoring by emphasizing
the importance of real-time visibility, data accuracy, and proactive intervention in project execution.
Like Umana et al.'s framework, Project Track PH uses centralized data monitoring to ensure timely
reporting, stakeholder engagement, and accountability in government-led initiatives, ultimately
promoting transparency and efficiency in project delivery.

Utilizing Data Platform Management to Implement "5W" Analysis Framework for
Preventing and Controlling Corruption in Grassroots Government

         The study by Zeyu Wang et al. (2024) presents a comprehensive approach that combines data
platform management with the "5W" (Who, What, When, Where, Why) analytical framework to
promote transparency, accountability, and corruption prevention in local government operations. The
researchers developed a data platform that collects and analyzes information from various
administrative processes, allowing for continuous supervision and evaluation of government
performance. By integrating the 5W model with SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities,
Threats) and PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act) cycles, the framework provides a systematic and data-driven
process for detecting inefficiencies, irregularities, and governance risks in real time.

         Findings from the study indicate that the use of data platforms in conjunction with structured
analysis models can significantly improve governance efficiency and public trust. The implementation of
the 5W-based data management system led to measurable outcomes, including a 23.24% increase in
governance performance scores, better transparency in public resource allocation, and stronger citizen
engagement. This highlights how integrating technology and analytical tools into government systems
can address long-standing issues such as corruption, poor oversight, and lack of accountability.

2.3 Synthesis of Literature

         The literature establishes a clear pattern: the demand for transparency in Philippine public spending is
high, driven by persistent governance gaps. The government's response has been a consistent pivot toward digital
solutions, evidenced by the rollout of e-Government master plans, the DIME project, and the exploration of
cutting-edge technologies like geotagging and blockchain.

                                                                                                                                                 13
         Patterns and Commonalities: All reviewed literature emphasizes the need for systems that integrate
physical accomplishment with financial utilization and the necessity of citizen feedback mechanisms to promote
genuine public accountability.

         Contradictions and Limitations: A key contradiction is the presence of multiple digital M&E efforts
alongside persistent issues of fragmentation and a lack of a single source of truth (Open Government Partnership,
n.d.-a). While geotagging proves effective, challenges remain, such as weak satellite signals in remote areas and
compatibility issues, highlighting the digital divide and technical constraints (Magwili et al., 2025).

         Gaps in Existing Research/Systems: The most critical gap is the lack of a unified, centralized digital
platform that synthesizes the fragmented data streams from different agencies, focuses on real-time milestone
and timeline monitoring with automated alerts (beyond just periodic progress reporting) to enable proactive
course correction, and fully integrates an audit trail to ensure traceability and unalterable accountability of all
user actions, a feature that aligns with the principles of blockchain-based immutability.

         This is the gap that Project Track PH: Government Implementation & Monitoring aims to fill by creating a
centralized, real-time platform with robust milestone and timeline tracking, supported by an auditable data store.

Chapter III. Methodology

3.1 S-M-A-R-T Method

3.1.1 Specific

         1.1 Identify and document each government project milestone with clear labels and responsible agencies.
         1.2 Define timeline phases per milestone, including dependencies and expected durations.
         1.3 Specify integration points with other microservices to ensure synchronized tracking and data flow.

3.1.2. Measurable

         2.1 Track project progress weekly, ensuring that the project will be finished on time.
         2.2 Create a plan and assign tasks to the stakeholders so that the tasks can be done smoothly.
         2.3 Log timeline deviations and calculate delay percentages per project.

3.1.3. Actionable

         3.1 Develop and assign SQL-based milestone tracking tasks to team members, ensuring each task has a
         clear deadline and scope.
         3.2 Build a system that allows authorized users to update milestone status in real time.
         3.3 Design and deploy an automated alert mechanism that flags overdue milestones and timeline
         deviations based on predefined thresholds.

3.1.4. Realistic

         4.1 Before going deeper, choose only the most important government projects to monitor so the team
         doesn't get overwhelmed.
         4.2 Use available data from official sources to avoid collecting everything manually.
         4.3 Set a regular schedule, like weekly, for updating milestone progress so it's consistent and manageable.

3.1.5. Timebound

         5.1 Complete assigned tasks within 3 days to allow finalization within the week.
         5.2 Finalize project documentation before the end of the semester.
         5.3 Ensure each chapter of the documentation is completed weekly to stay on track.

3.5 Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT)

         The Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) was applied to estimate task durations for the
Milestone & Timeline Monitoring microservice. PERT incorporates uncertainty by considering three possible
values for each activity: Optimistic (O), Most Likely (M), and Pessimistic (P). These values are combined using the
weighted average formula:

emphasizes the most likely duration while accounting for potential variation.

Justification for Using 4 and 6 in the PERT Formula

Mathematical Foundation

The PERT (Program Evaluation and Review Technique) formula uses the weighted average:
TE = (O + 4M + P) / 6
The coefficients 4 and 6 are not arbitrary values but are mathematically derived from the Beta probability
distribution, which has been proven to accurately model project task durations under uncertainty.

Why 4? (Weight for Most Likely)

The coefficient 4 represents the relative probability weight assigned to the Most Likely (M) estimate. This
weighting reflects that:

    1. Statistical Probability: Under normal working conditions, the Most Likely scenario occurs approximately
         68% of the time, while Optimistic and Pessimistic scenarios each occur roughly 16% of the time. The 1:4:1
         ratio approximates this probability distribution.

    2. Beta Distribution Mean Approximation: The PERT formula approximates the mean () of a Beta
         distribution, where the mode (most likely value) has significantly higher probability density than the
         extreme values. Mathematical derivation of the Beta distribution mean produces this 4x weighting factor.

    3. Balancing Realism with Risk: By assigning 4 times the weight to the Most Likely estimate, the formula
         prevents extreme scenarios (best-case or worst-case) from dominating the calculation while still
         accounting for project uncertainty.

                                                                                                                                                 27
Why 6? (Denominator)

The denominator 6 is the sum of all probability weights in the formula:
Weight of O + Weight of M + Weight of P = 1 + 4 + 1 = 6
This ensures the formula produces a proper weighted average, wher e all weights sum to a whole number that
maintains mathematical consistency.

Statistical Validation

The PERT formula with these specific coefficients has been empirically validated across thousands of projects
since its development by the U.S. Navy in 1958 for the Polaris missile program. Research demonstrates that this
weighting produces estimates within 5-10% accuracy of actual task durations, significantly outperforming simple
averaging methods (O + M + P) / 3 by 30-40% (Malcolm et al., 1959).

Alternative Formulas and Why They Fail

Formula                 Limitation

(O + M + P) / 3 Gives equal weight to all estimates, ignoring that M is most probable

(O + 2M + P) / 4 Underweights the Most Likely estimate, producing less accurate results

(O + 5M + P) / 7 Overweights M, making extreme scenarios almost irrelevant

Only the 1:4:1 weighting ratio (producing the divisor of 6) accurately approximates the Beta distribution mean.

Application in This Project

For the Milestone & Timeline Monitoring microservice, the PERT formula with coefficients 4 and 6 provides
realistic time estimates that account for both typical working conditions and potential risks or efficiencies. For
example:

Backend Development:

     O = 15 days, M = 21 days, P = 30 days
     TE = (15 + 4(21) + 30) / 6 = (15 + 84 + 30) / 6 = 129 / 6 = 21.5 days

3.16 Acquisition & Outsourcing Policy

Purpose

         This policy explains how our team will acquire and outsource public project data for the Milestone and
Timeline Monitoring microservice. Since our system tracks government milestones and timelines, we rely on
verified records from official sources. If needed, we are prepared to present the system to government agencies
to formally request access to these records. The goal is to ensure that all data used in the system is accurate,
authorized, and aligned with our mission to promote transparency and accountability.

Scope

         The policy applies to all activities related to getting public records, project timelines, and milestone data
from government agencies or external providers. It also covers outsourcing tasks like formatting, integration, or
data conversion when needed.

Acquisition Process

         Before we request any data, we first identify which government projects are important to monitor. We
define what kind of milestone and timeline data we need and make sure it matches our SMART objectives and
backend logic. If we need to formally request data, we prepare a Request for Proposal (RFP) that explains what we
need, how often we need it, and in what format. This RFP is sent to the agency or provider, and we track their
response to see if it meets our requirements.

         If we're choosing between multiple providers, we use methods like sole sourcing (only one provider
available), single sourcing (we choose one among many), request for quotations (RFQ), or sealed bidding (if
required). Before we work with any vendor, we screen them by asking them to fill out a vendor questionnaire,
checking references from other NGOs or agencies, and making sure they're not listed on any restricted or banned
lists. Only vendors who pass this screening can supply data or services.

Outsourcing Process

                                                                                                                                                 71
         We outsource tasks when the data we need isn't available locally, when formatting requires special tools,
or when our team doesn't have enough time to handle everything. Outsourcing can be done onshore (within the
Philippines), nearshore (within Southeast Asia), or offshore (international providers with good records).
Outsourcing helps us get data faster, reduce our workload, and access specialized tools. But it also comes with
risks like slower response times, data errors, and less control over delivery. To manage these risks, we use clear
contracts, ask for sample data before full engagement, and schedule regular updates with the provider.

Documentation and Audit

         Every acquisition and outsourcing activity must be documented. This includes the source of the data,
contact details, method used, screening results, and how the data was integrated. These records are stored in our
system's audit trail and linked to the milestone and progress report modules. We also validate all data before
using it in the system.

Approval and Review

         All sourcing decisions must be approved by the Project Manager and recorded in the Communication
Plan. This policy will be reviewed every semester or whenever we make major changes to the system. By
following this policy, we make sure that all data used in our microservice is reliable, traceable, and supports our
goal of improving transparency in government project monitoring.
